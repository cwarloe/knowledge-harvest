  • 1A capture is a recording of packets, in order, as they crossed one point on the network.
  • 2Wireshark opens the file and shows you every packet.
  • 3tshark is the same program at the command line.
Open the file in Wireshark with File → Open, or drag it onto the window.
STEP 2Click a packet to see inside it
  • 1Click a packet in the list.
  • 2Its layers appear in the details tree.
  • 3Click a layer and its bytes light up in the bytes pane.
Three panes: the packet list, the details tree, and the raw bytes.
STEP 3Type a display filter
  • 1Type in the filter bar. Red means incomplete or invalid. Green means valid.
  • 2Press Enter (or click Apply).
Example: tcp.port == 42876 keeps only packets using that port.
STEP 3bNothing was deleted
In the file: 20 packets
On screen: 10
A filter only changes what you see. The capture file is untouched.
  • 1Click Clear, next to the filter bar, to bring every packet back.
Filtering hides packets. It never removes them from the capture.
STEP 4Combine two conditions with and
  • 1and: both must be true.
  • 2or: either one is enough.
  • 3Here: the port, and the SYN flag set. That's the two packets that open the connection.
Combining filters narrows the view to exactly the packets you mean.
STEP 4bSymbols work too
  • 1&& means and. || means or.
  • 2> compares: tcp.len > 0 keeps packets that carry data.
Both packets here carry data: the request and the reply.

The stream joins the packets of one conversation back into what the client said and what the server said.
