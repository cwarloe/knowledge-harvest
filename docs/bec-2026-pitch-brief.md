# Knowledge Harvest
**Turn your best people’s know-how into better performance across the team.**

## Customer and costly problem
Our proposed first customers are staffing and recruiting businesses with roughly 50–200 internal employees. The buyer is an owner or operations leader responsible for recruiting or account performance. The best fit has a recurring, valuable task that experienced employees handle well while others struggle—even after learning the software and documented process. Comparable service businesses are a later market.  
Employees may know the clicks but miss the cues, questions, and judgment that make the process work. The result can be slow execution, rework, repeated expert interruptions, and inconsistent client service. **Illustrative scale:** ten employees spending 20 avoidable minutes per workday represents about 733 hours annually, or $25,667 of capacity at an assumed loaded labor cost of $35/hour. These are assumptions, not measured customer losses.

## Service and main benefit
Knowledge Harvest is a performance consulting service. We diagnose one costly workflow, combine existing documentation with expert demonstrations and focused questions, and turn the useful reasoning into guidance, realistic practice, feedback, and mentoring in the flow of work. We also identify barriers that require a process, access, tooling, or management change. AI assists with transcription, analysis, and drafting; experts review the result.  
The main benefit is helping employees perform that workflow correctly with less avoidable delay and expert dependence. In the illustrative team above, recovering half the assumed wasted time would release about 367 hours of capacity annually. The engagement measures actual task quality and time; it does not promise this improvement.

## Alternatives and differentiation
Alternatives include manager-led mentoring, courses, SOPs, capture tools, AI knowledge platforms, and specialist workflow-learning consultants. Our proposed distinction is a tightly scoped service for staffing teams: connect expert judgment, existing resources, mentor support, and task-level measurement in the client’s current tools. This positioning is a hypothesis; the underlying principles are established.

## Business model and founder fit
Revenue comes from a paid diagnosis and separately scoped implementation. Working price hypotheses are $1,450 for diagnosis and $6,000 for implementation, subject to scope. Charles brings corporate training experience, a master’s in adult learning technology, and firsthand experience with exceptional staffing performers and mentor development. Knowledge Harvest has a capture prototype and the Network Analyst Development Framework provides related learning design work. Paid demand, pricing, and repeatable client results for this offer remain unvalidated.

## 90-second pitch script
About 187 words. Rehearse at a natural pace; trim if it exceeds 90 seconds.

In a staffing company where I worked, one account manager built an account that led the company in profitability for five consecutive years. We taught others the systems and procedures, but struggled to transfer the judgment that made him successful.  
That’s the problem Knowledge Harvest addresses.  
Our first customers are staffing businesses where experienced employees consistently handle important work better than newer colleagues. Their leaders need more people who can perform, while protecting their experts’ time.  
We start with one costly workflow and find out why people struggle. We combine existing documentation, recordings of experts doing real work, and questions that reveal what they notice and why they act.  
Then we build guidance, realistic practice, and mentor support employees can use on the job. Sometimes the right answer is a process change rather than another course.  
AI helps us capture and organize the knowledge. Our value is helping the company put it to work and measure the result.  
We charge for diagnosis and scoped implementation. I bring corporate training experience and a master’s in adult learning technology. Knowledge Harvest helps companies turn individual expertise into team performance.

## Viability judgment
**Credible as a focused consulting venture.** The evidence supports the need for workplace support and the existence of competing commercial services. It does not yet establish willingness to pay for this particular offer, its delivery margins, or its effect on customer performance.

The opportunity is to solve a business execution problem that falls between the client’s documents, software, training, and informal mentoring. Better AI lowers some production effort and also strengthens competitors. A buyer could assemble these tools internally; the reason to hire Charles is accountable diagnosis and implementation when the business lacks the time or learning design capacity to do that well.  
The offer should remain useful even when capture is already done. A client can bring Scribe guides, videos, SOPs, or AI-generated materials. The question becomes: can employees use those resources to make the right decisions in real cases?

### A concrete first engagement
Choose one recruiting workflow, such as clarifying a requisition and deciding which candidates merit submission. Compare expert and less experienced performance on comparable cases. Check whether differences reflect transferable judgment or advantages such as account history, candidate access, authority, workload, or tools.  
Review selected recordings with the expert: What caught your attention? What did you expect? What alternative did you rule out? What would change your decision? Create a concise decision aid, several contrasting practice cases, and prompts mentors can use during live work. Put the aid where employees already work and agree who keeps it current.  
Assess improvement using task time, submission quality, rework, or expert escalations. Track client and market conditions so a favorable change is not automatically attributed to the service. Revenue outcomes can matter, but they are often influenced by factors beyond training.

## Simple economics for an advisor conversation
The $1,450 diagnostic comes from an earlier proposal, not an accepted sale. The $6,000 implementation figure is illustrative. They make the business model concrete without pretending price validation.

> * **Customer example:** 10 employees × 20 minutes/day ÷ 60 × 220 workdays × $35/hour = $25,667 annual capacity value. Recovering half equals approximately $12,833. This is capacity, not automatic cash savings or profit.  
> * **Combined illustrative fee:** $7,450. If the assumed improvement occurs throughout a comparable full year, capacity value exceeds the fee by about $5,383, before client participation, adoption effort, and maintenance costs.  
> * **Provider scope check:** $6,000 implementation minus an assumed $300 direct cost, divided by 50 total hours, yields $114 per hour before overhead and taxes. Include sales, revisions, meetings, and follow-up in those hours. At 80 hours, it falls to about $71.

These examples show why one workflow, explicit deliverables, and limited revision scope matter. They are not a forecast, ROI promise, or recommended investment decision.

### Three likely advisor questions
**Why would someone pay?** Because the workflow is costly and existing resources are not producing reliable execution. The diagnosis must connect that cost to a barrier the engagement can actually address.  
**Why can’t they just use AI?** Some can. Our customer lacks the capacity to diagnose the problem, validate expert reasoning, integrate guidance and mentoring, and follow through on adoption.  
**What evidence do you already have?** Relevant founder experience, prototype and learning design work, transfer research, and current competing services. No paid validation or measured outcomes for this new package should be claimed.
