# Knowledge Harvest: Business Hypothesis

_Last updated: October 2026_

## Core hypothesis

**Knowledge Harvest helps growing founder-led companies transfer the know-how behind their success to employees so the business can scale without unnecessary founder dependency.**

The problem is not simply that knowledge is undocumented. A founder may have documented the routine process and trained capable employees, yet unusual estimates, difficult customers, quality decisions, troubleshooting, and exceptions still return to the founder.

Knowledge Harvest focuses on **capability dependency**: the employee has responsibility but cannot yet recognize, judge, troubleshoot, or apply standards at the level required to perform independently.

## Customer hypothesis

The initial customer is a proven founder-led service business that has already hired a team. Employees can handle routine work, but economically important exceptions still require the founder.

The stronger signal is not a particular employee count. It is recurring escalation behavior:

- "Can you look at this?"
- "What should I do here?"
- "Before this goes out, can you review it?"
- "I followed the process, but this situation is different."
- "It's faster if I just do it myself."

Initial beachhead hypothesis: **MSPs and technical service firms**. Specialty contractors such as HVAC are a strong adjacent market.

## What Knowledge Harvest does — and does not do

Founder dependency can have several causes:

| Dependency | Typical solution | Knowledge Harvest role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural | Operations, EOS, COO | Usually refer/coordinate |
| Relationship | Sales/account transition | Usually refer/coordinate |
| Capacity | Staffing/resourcing | Not the solution |
| Capability | Knowledge and performance transfer | Core focus |

If Sarah is not authorized to approve an estimate, that is a decision-rights problem. If Sarah is authorized but still asks Dave because she cannot tell whether an unusual estimate is good, that is a Knowledge Harvest problem.

## Method

### 1. Find
Identify recurring founder/expert interventions and work that waits, returns, or gets corrected.

### 2. Harvest
Use actual incidents and work artifacts to uncover what the expert noticed, knew, expected, decided, rejected, or judged.

### 3. Transfer
Build the smallest intervention that enables performance. It may be a decision guide, job aid, examples/rubric, scenario practice, coaching, documentation, automation, AI support, an authority change, or formal training.

### 4. Prove
Test independent performance on routine cases and exceptions. Reduce avoidable escalation without increasing errors, rework, callbacks, or customer problems.

## Capability being transferred

Knowledge Harvest is concerned with more than procedural knowledge:

- knowledge
- skill
- judgment
- standards ("what good looks like")
- troubleshooting and situational awareness
- knowing when escalation is genuinely appropriate

**Internal principle: Don't document the founder. Reproduce the capability.**

## Initial offer hypothesis

### Escalation Audit
Review a defined period of founder interventions and produce a **Founder Dependency Map**:

- what escalated;
- why it escalated;
- business impact;
- whether it is reasonably transferable; and
- the smallest likely intervention.

### Capability Transfer Sprint
Select one expensive recurring dependency, harvest the relevant expertise, implement the intervention, test employee performance, and measure the before/after result.

Expansion happens dependency-by-dependency after measurable value is demonstrated.

Pricing is intentionally not fixed yet; willingness to pay and delivery economics remain validation questions.

## Measurement

The primary outcome is not documents produced. Candidate measures include:

- avoidable founder/expert escalations per week;
- founder/expert time consumed by escalation and review;
- rework, callbacks, reopened work, errors, or quality defects;
- resolution/cycle time;
- employee ramp-to-independence; and
- decision quality on realistic exceptions, including correct escalation.

## Role of AI

AI is an enabling technology, not the product.

Growing firms already produce evidence of failed handoffs in tickets, messages, estimates, revisions, calls, recordings, SOPs, and other work artifacts. AI can make rigorous expertise-transfer work economical for smaller companies by helping identify patterns, surface candidate incidents, organize evidence, and support targeted SME elicitation.

The performance methodology determines **what should be transferred and how**.

## Competitive frame

| Alternative | Primary job |
| --- | --- |
| Scribe/process capture | Record observable steps |
| SOP/systemization consultant | Document and standardize operations |
| AI knowledge base | Retrieve organizational information |
| EOS/fractional COO | Structure, ownership, accountability, decision rights |
| Training/LMS | Teach defined knowledge and skills |
| Expertise elicitation / CTA tools | Extract expert cognition |
| **Knowledge Harvest** | **Diagnose dependency and transfer measurable capability** |

These are not necessarily competitors to exclude. Many can become tools or referral partners inside a Knowledge Harvest engagement.

## Key validation questions

1. Do founders recognize capability dependency as a distinct problem worth purchasing?
2. Which beachhead combines painful escalation, measurable outcomes, accessible work artifacts, and willingness to pay?
3. How much expert/consultant time is required to transfer one meaningful capability?
4. What entry price creates commitment without making the diagnostic difficult to buy?
5. Which acquisition channel works first: direct founder outreach, industry networks, operations/EOS/COO referrals, or exit/valuation advisers?
6. What becomes defensible over time: diagnostic taxonomy, industry patterns, outcome data, software workflow, or a combination?
