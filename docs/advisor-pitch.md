# Advisor Pitch: Knowledge Harvest

## 2–3 minute version

I'm exploring a business called **Knowledge Harvest** that helps growing founder-led companies solve a particular scaling problem.

Imagine someone who has spent years building a successful technical service company. Early on, the founder does everything and learns how to estimate jobs, handle difficult customers, troubleshoot unusual problems, and recognize what good work looks like.

Then the company succeeds and hires people.

The founder documents processes. The employees know the routine work. But everything unusual still comes back:

> "Can you look at this estimate?"  
> "What should I do with this customer?"  
> "I followed the process, but this situation is different."

The founder has become a bottleneck, but the answer isn't necessarily another SOP or another training course. Some of what made the company successful still exists in the founder's experience, judgment, troubleshooting, and standards.

**That's the problem Knowledge Harvest addresses.**

We start with the actual situations that still escalate to the founder and determine why employees need the founder. Sometimes it's missing information, authority, or a broken process. Sometimes it is capability: the employee does not yet recognize the cues, make the judgment, troubleshoot the exception, or apply the founder's standard.

Then we build the smallest appropriate intervention. It might be a decision guide, job aid, realistic practice, coaching, better documentation, automation, AI support, or actual training.

The goal isn't more content. **The goal is transferred capability.**

We then test whether the employee can handle situations that previously escalated without increasing errors, callbacks, rework, or customer problems.

AI makes this especially interesting now. Small companies already create evidence of these failed handoffs in tickets, messages, estimates, corrections, calls, recordings, and other work artifacts. AI can help find patterns and make expertise-transfer methods that have traditionally been labor-intensive practical for smaller businesses.

I'm initially looking at founder-led MSPs and technical service firms, with specialty contractors as a strong adjacent market, because the dependency is visible, expensive, and measurable.

The initial offer could be an **Escalation Audit**: identify where the business still depends unnecessarily on the founder, then choose one expensive dependency and prove that the capability can be transferred.

The question I'm validating isn't whether companies need another AI knowledge-management tool.

It's:

**"I've hired people to help me scale. Why do they still need me to make the business work?"**

## Questions for an advisor

- Is this sufficiently distinct as a purchasing problem, or should it live inside an existing category?
- Which assumption should be validated before entering the entrepreneurship competition?
- Is founder-led technical services narrow enough for an initial customer?
- What evidence of willingness to pay is credible at this stage?
- Is the Escalation Audit a credible low-risk entry offer?
- What would make this competition-worthy rather than simply a good consulting practice?
