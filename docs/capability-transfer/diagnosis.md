# Diagnose the dependency

Start with an actual incident and ask the employee for their account before accepting the founder's explanation. More than one cause may apply. Classification is a working hypothesis until evidence supports it.

| Possible cause | Question / evidence to seek | Smallest plausible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge | Can the employee locate and explain the relevant information? | Make the current source accessible; give a concise explanation |
| Skill | Can they execute the action in a supported attempt? | Demonstration, practice, feedback, suitable tools |
| Judgment or standards | Can they distinguish two superficially similar cases and justify their choice? | Contrasting examples, decision aid, exception practice |
| Authority | Can they make the right decision but need permission anyway? | Owner clarifies decision rights and limits |
| Access or tools | Are the current contract, system, or equipment available when needed? | Restore access or fix the work system |
| Capacity | Is there realistic time and attention for the task? | Supervisor changes workload or coverage |
| Expectations or incentives | Is initiative rewarded in words but reversed or penalized in practice? | Align feedback, review practices, and actual expectations |
| Relationship dependency | Does the customer insist on the founder despite competent employee performance? | Account transition; coordinate outside capability work |
| Structural dependency | Does work require founder approval because roles or ownership are undefined? | Operations intervention; refer or coordinate |

For each cause record: incident IDs, employee account, expert account, competing explanation, confidence (unconfirmed / supported / contradicted), human confirmation, selected action, owner, due date, and resolution evidence. Avoid a numerical diagnostic score that implies validation.

## Conditions to check before live application

These prompts adapt the concerns covered by the Institute's transfer framework; they are Knowledge Harvest working prompts, not its licensed audit or a validated questionnaire. See [sources](sources.md).

| Condition | Concrete prompt | Typical owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desire to apply | What useful outcome does the employee want from this change? | Employee |
| Confidence | Which part can they handle, and where are they unsure? Compare with actual attempts. | Employee + reviewer |
| Persistence | If the first attempt fails or a shift gets busy, what is the fallback and next opportunity? | Employee + supervisor |
| Clear expectations | What action and quality standard are expected after the pilot? | Supervisor |
| Relevance | Which current work cases need this capability? | Expert |
| Active practice | Where will the employee make the decision rather than watch it? | Pilot lead |
| Application plan | On which suitable work item, when, and with what aid will they act? | Employee |
| Opportunity | Is eligible work actually expected to occur? What if it does not? | Supervisor |
| Capacity | What time, access, and workload coverage are available? | Supervisor |
| Supervisor support | Who reviews the first attempts and gives actionable feedback? | Supervisor |
| Peer support | Who can help without quietly taking over the decision? | Supervisor + peer |
| Organizational expectations | Will management accept decisions inside the agreed authority boundary? | Business owner |

Mark each condition **ready**, **blocked**, **unknown**, or **not applicable with reason**. Every blocked/unknown condition needs an owner and next action. Missing authority, essential access, or a safe review/fallback route blocks live application. Other gaps require an explicit, documented judgment by the supervisor; do not hide them in an average score.

## Example diagnostic fork

An employee escalates a contract question. They correctly classify a fresh case using the contract but say only the founder may communicate exclusions. Confirm the policy with the owner. Change authority and communication support first. If they also misread a boundary case, add targeted judgment practice. Recording another general walkthrough does not resolve the permission barrier.
