# Sources and design rationale

Reviewed November 6, 2026. This is Knowledge Harvest's proposed delivery and product framework. It has not been validated with customers or as a competence assessment. The fictional example does not constitute outcome evidence.

## External references

- [Institute for Transfer Effectiveness — 12 Levers](https://www.transfereffectiveness.com/en/eleveroftransfereffectiveness): organizes transfer conditions around trainees, training design, and organization. This informs the condition prompts; Knowledge Harvest's workflow, templates, gates, and metrics are our own design choices.
- [Institute — Transfer Toolbox](https://www.transfereffectiveness.com/toolbox): describes practical transfer tools and templates. This framework does not reproduce its paid templates, audit questions, certification materials, or tool library.
- [trevato — partnership with Kirkpatrick Partners and the Institute](https://trevato.com/partnerships/kirkpatrick-ite/): emphasizes connecting evaluation, transfer, multiple perspectives, and business indicators over time. This informs the separation of work evidence and business outcomes; it does not establish effectiveness of Knowledge Harvest.

The 12 Levers of Transfer Effectiveness name is attributed to the Institute. No affiliation, certification, license, or endorsement is implied. The homepage's broad 20% application claim is not used as a quantitative assumption, sales promise, or pilot benchmark. External sources support design rationale; they do not validate invented success thresholds or guarantee causal effects.

## Questions a pilot must answer

Can the owner recognize a dependency worth addressing? Can an employee use the harvested reasoning on fresh work? Can quality and appropriate escalation be maintained? Does saved founder attention outweigh ongoing support and delivery effort? Which manual steps recur enough to justify software? These remain empirical questions, not framework assumptions.
