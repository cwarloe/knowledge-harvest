# Worked example: service-agreement decisions

**Entirely fictional.** Names, policies, dates, observations, and numbers illustrate the framework; they are not customer results or general contract advice.

## Find and diagnose

Pilot KH-DEMO-001: employee Alex classifies requests as covered support, quoted project work, or requiring escalation. Founder Morgan is the expert and reviewer; supervisor Sam schedules the work. Morgan's dual role limits reviewer independence.

Boundary: ordinary support requests with a current agreement available. Exclude legal disputes, security incidents, and customers without a current agreement. Always escalate ambiguous wording, requested commercial concessions, and requests outside Alex's delegated authority.

Baseline: ten eligible items in a two-week fictional window. Six reached Morgan: four avoidable and two appropriate. All ten were reviewed; eight met the quality standard. Founder handling and review took 90 minutes; peer support took 10. No required escalation was missed. Logs include all ten items, not just the six escalations.

Alex can find the agreement but confuses restoring an existing service with adding new scope. Alex also believes every exclusion message needs founder approval. Morgan confirms this expectation has been inconsistent. Diagnosis: supported judgment gap plus authority/expectation gap. A generic recording alone would miss the latter.

## Harvest and prepare

Morgan contrasts three fictional cases:

| Case | Relevant evidence | Approved decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restore an existing mailbox | Agreement includes support for the existing mailbox service | Handle within the documented support boundary |
| Add a new branch-office setup | Current agreement excludes new-site projects | Route for a project quote; no price commitment |
| Replace failing hardware | Agreement wording is ambiguous about hardware procurement | Escalate with the relevant clause and missing information |

Aid v1 mistakenly says “mailbox work is covered.” On an unfamiliar practice case involving a new mail migration, Alex chooses covered support. Morgan identifies the overbroad rule. They revise the aid to v2: identify current service, proposed change, applicable clause, explicit exclusions, and delegated action. Ambiguous wording stops the decision. Source references and Morgan's dated approval accompany v2.

Alex then works three fresh cases: a routine restoration, a new-scope request, and an ambiguity requiring escalation. Expected answers are withheld until the initial attempt. Morgan records reasoning and feedback. This establishes **demonstrated in practice**, not workplace independence.

## Apply

Morgan explicitly delegates routine classification and approved wording to Alex, excluding commitments on price or concessions. Sam provides contract access, time during the next shift, and an after-work review. A peer can locate documents but must record any help interpreting them. If no eligible request appears, use the next suitable shift and extend the evidence window rather than inventing live-work results.

Pilot evidence agreement: review all ten follow-up opportunities, include the three task categories where available, and perform a later quality check. Critical customer-impacting misclassification or missed mandatory escalation pauses delegated decisions pending review. These are fictional task-specific choices, not validated universal thresholds.

## Prove — illustrative arithmetic

Follow-up: ten comparable eligible items over two weeks. Nine meet the standard. One noncritical misclassification is corrected before customer communication. Three items escalate: one avoidable and two appropriate. Seven outputs are completed accurately without unplanned help; two other acceptable outputs involved help. Founder time is 45 minutes and peer support 20 minutes. No required escalation is missed. A delayed callback review is still pending.

| Metric | Baseline | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avoidable escalations | 4/10 = 40% | 1/10 = 10% |
| Required escalations missed | 0/10; 2 cases required escalation | 0/10; 2 cases required escalation |
| Acceptable reviewed outputs | 8/10 = 80% | 9/10 = 90% |
| Independent acceptable outputs | Not captured; cannot compare | 7/10 = 70% |
| Founder minutes per eligible item | 90/10 = 9 | 45/10 = 4.5 |
| Total support minutes per eligible item | (90+10)/10 = 10 | (45+20)/10 = 6.5 |

Delivery effort is separate: expert preparation 60 minutes, employee practice 45, supervisor setup 20, consultant facilitation/documentation 180. Do not subtract these once-only costs silently from recurring support minutes or promise a payback period from ten cases.

Conclusion: encouraging descriptive evidence, not causal proof. The intervention combined an aid, practice, clearer authority, and access. Independent acceptable work was observed on seven cases; this does not establish independence across the full task class. Owner chooses **continue with revision**: inspect the misclassification, gather missing variety if necessary, complete the delayed review, and then reassess the agreed evidence requirement. Preserve the unsuccessful practice attempt and live correction.
